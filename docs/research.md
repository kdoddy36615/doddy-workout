# PPL Tracker: program and injury research

_Researched 2026-11-07. Covers the source program, StrongLifts comparison, three injuries, and proposed program edits._

> **Not medical advice.** This is a literature summary written to shape a training app. None of it is a diagnosis. Kevin's shoulder and hip and Emily's abdomen have not been formally assessed. Anything in the red-flag boxes means stop and see a clinician (GP, sports-medicine doctor or physio). Where a recommendation is the author's own reasoning rather than something a source states, it is tagged **[reasoning, no direct source]**.

---

## 1. Summary

- **The handoff's main-lift structure matches the original Reddit PPL.** Deadlift 1x5+, squat 2x5 + 1x5+, alternating bench/OHP 4x5 + 1x5+ with the other press at 3x8-12, rows and deadlifts alternating on pull days, and the 3-fails-then-deload-10% rule all come from the source. Most deviations are in accessory volume, the injury-driven swaps, and a smaller deadlift increment.
- **Two factual corrections.** The original was posted in **2015**, not 2017, and the author's username is spelled both "Metallicdpa" and "metallicadpa" in mirrors. Its deadlift progression is **+10 lb per session**, not +5.
- **Kevin, shoulder.** The landmine-for-OHP swap fits the main aggravating factor in AAOS guidance (overhead lifting). The biggest gap in the handoff is **deadlift grip**. In two video studies, all the deadlift biceps ruptures in one and 56 of 57 ruptures in the other happened with the forearm *supinated*, and none with it pronated. Use double overhand, hook grip or straps instead.
- **Kevin, hip.** A box squat is reasonable. **Sprinting and running should be excluded**, since hip-flexor activity rises sharply with running speed. Leg-press depth, dead-bug lever length and hanging leg raises also need limits.
- **Emily, abdomen.** Treat it conservatively for 2-4 weeks. That means **no AMRAP sets, no sprint intervals, and regressed core work** (a short-lever side plank in particular). She should check daily for hernia red flags, and the most important one is a **lump or bulge**.
- **The app needs a pain log that can block progression.** Pain-monitored loading is how the tendinopathy literature handles this. It also gives the app a concrete rule to implement.

**Original days** ([archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/))

- **Pull:** Deadlift 1x5+ *alternating with* barbell row 4x5, 1x5+. Then pulldowns, pull-ups or chin-ups 3x8-12; seated cable row or chest-supported row 3x8-12; face pulls 5x15-20; hammer curls 4x8-12; DB curls 4x8-12.
- **Push:** Bench 4x5, 1x5+ *alternating with* OHP 4x5, 1x5+. The other press follows at 3x8-12. Then incline DB press 3x8-12; triceps pushdown 3x8-12 supersetted with lateral raise 3x15-20; overhead triceps extension 3x8-12 supersetted with lateral raise 3x15-20.
- **Legs:** Squat 2x5, 1x5+; RDL 3x8-12; leg press 3x8-12; leg curl 3x8-12; calf raise 5x8-12.

**Original rules** ([archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/), [Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/metallicadpappl))

- **Main lifts:** +2.5 kg / 5 lb per session for upper-body lifts and squat; **+5 kg / 10 lb per session for deadlift**.
- **AMRAP:** perform with good form, not to failure. Liftosaur adds "stop when bar speed slows significantly."
- **Failure:** misses caused by bad sleep, food or recovery don't count. Fail the same weight **3 sessions in a row**, then deload about **10%** and build back up.
- **Accessories:** at 3x12 with good form, add weight. If you can't stay in 8-12 reps, reduce the weight.
- **Start weights:** work up from the empty bar in sets of 5 until bar speed slows, then back off 5 lb.
- **Warm-up:** ramp sets of the lift itself. Foam rolling and dynamic work are fine; **avoid static stretching before lifting**.
- **Rest:** 3-5 min before the first exercise, 1-3 min between the rest.
- **Allowed substitutions:** the main lifts (squat, bench, deadlift, OHP, bent-over row) are fixed, and Smith machine versions don't count. Accessories can be swapped, and the listed options include **incline DB press → landmine press**, face pulls → rear-delt flies or band pull-aparts, any curl variation, and leg press → front squat.
- **Abs:** "a few sets of weighted planks, ab wheel, or hanging leg raises" on squat or deadlift days.
- **Cutting:** drop accessories from 3 sets to 2 if recovery suffers.
- _A "don't AMRAP bench and OHP on the same day" rule appears only in user-made Jefit copies, so treat it as a community variant, not part of the original._

## 3. Kevin: right long-head biceps tendinopathy

**Mechanism.** The long head of the biceps attaches to the top of the shoulder socket. Tendinitis usually comes from wear and repetitive use, and repeated overhead motion raises the risk ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/biceps-tendinitis/)). The long head is a recognized source of anterior shoulder pain, but its biomechanical role is still debated ([Krupp, JOSPT 2009](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2009.2802)).

**Symptoms that should match.** Pain at the front of the shoulder that is worse with overhead lifting; pain reaching backward (seatbelt, coat); ache down the upper arm; occasional snapping ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/biceps-tendinitis/)).

**Evidence on management**
- Evidence for conservative care is thin. A 2024 scoping review found "a dearth of evidence" on the best approach ([Brazilian J Phys Ther 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38219522/)).
- An international physio Delphi panel agreed on these points ([IJSPT 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159730/)):
  - progressive isometric, concentric and eccentric loading of the biceps;
  - progressive resistance work for the rotator cuff, serratus, middle and lower trapezius, rhomboids and lats;
  - loading matched to **tissue capacity and pain irritability**;
  - training modification.

  This is expert opinion from 29 panellists, not trial evidence.
- **Pain-monitored loading.** In an Achilles tendinopathy RCT, continuing to train under a pain-monitoring model did no harm compared with rest ([Silbernagel, AJSM 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17307888/)). A later protocol built on that model advises an extra rest day if pain is **above 5/10 the day after** a session ([BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8364697/)). Both are Achilles studies, applied here by analogy.
- **Weight-room levers.** Grip, hand spacing, bar path, and start and finish positions can all be changed to keep an injured shoulder lifting ([Fees, AJSM 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9784824/)). Poor technique is a main extrinsic risk factor for shoulder injury from resistance training ([Kolber, JSCR 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20508476/)).
- **Mixed-grip deadlift.** Two YouTube video studies looked at *distal* biceps ruptures (at the elbow, not the long head). In one, every deadlift rupture occurred on the **supinated** arm of a mixed grip with the elbow straight ([Kapicioglu 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8237209/)). In the other, 56 of 57 cases (98%) had the forearm supinated, and none occurred with the forearm pronated ([Jukes, Bone & Joint Open 2022](https://online.boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/2633-1462.310.BJO-2022-0123.R1)). This is a different injury from Kevin's, but it is a cheap risk to remove from a biceps that is already irritated.

**Exercise-by-exercise review**

| Exercise | Concern | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| OHP → landmine press | Overhead lifting is the classic aggravator ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/biceps-tendinitis/)) | Keep the swap. The landmine press stays below full overhead reach **[reasoning, no direct source]**. As a MAIN lift, use +2.5 lb jumps, because added plates on a lever increase the load in uneven steps **[reasoning]**. |
| Bench, 45° elbow tuck | The bottom position puts the shoulder in extension, which stretches the long head **[reasoning]** | Keep the tuck. If the bottom pinches, use a floor press or a board or towel press. Limiting range of motion is a recognized modification ([Fees 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9784824/)). |
| Incline DB press | Pressing angle moves toward overhead; deep stretch at the bottom | Use a low incline (about 15-30°) and stop when the elbows are level with the torso **[reasoning; ROM lever per [Fees 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9784824/)]** |
| DB curl | Loads the irritated muscle directly | **Keep it.** Progressive biceps loading has Delphi consensus ([IJSPT 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159730/)). Start light, use a hammer (neutral) grip as the fallback, and **avoid incline or behind-the-body curls** (shoulder extension) **[reasoning]**. |
| Lat pulldown | A supinated close grip (chin-up style) makes the biceps work harder | Use a pronated or neutral grip **[reasoning]** |
| Seated cable row | Low risk | Keep |
| Face pull | Rotator cuff and scapular work is supported ([Delphi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159730/)) | Keep. Pull to chin or nose height with the elbows at or below shoulder height, and stop if the front of the shoulder pinches at end-range external rotation **[reasoning]**. |
| Lateral raise | Above-shoulder abduction | Keep it to shoulder height as planned |
| Triceps pushdown | Low risk | Keep. Don't add the original's **overhead** triceps extension **[reasoning: overhead position]**. |
| Deadlift | Mixed grip puts the supinated arm at risk | **Double overhand, then hook grip or straps** ([Kapicioglu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8237209/), [Jukes](https://online.boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/2633-1462.310.BJO-2022-0123.R1)) |
| Barbell row | Overhand grip is fine; underhand (Yates-style) rows load the biceps more | Overhand **[reasoning]** |
| Box squat (back squat) | A low-bar or wide grip pushes the shoulder into extension and external rotation | High bar with a comfortable, wider grip. Use a safety bar or goblet squat if the bar position hurts **[reasoning]**. |

**Warm-up (about 5 min, push and pull days).** Band external rotation, band pull-aparts, scapular wall slides kept below the pain point, then light biceps isometrics (hold a curl at 90°, 2-3 x 30 s at a load that is painless or mildly uncomfortable). This is consistent with Delphi consensus on isometric biceps loading and rotator cuff and scapular work ([IJSPT 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159730/)). The specific exercises are **[reasoning]**.

**Stop and modify rules**
- Pain above **3/10** during a set, or a sharp pinch: stop that exercise and use its regression. The 3/10 default is a conservative app choice; the literature ceiling is about 5/10 ([Silbernagel](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17307888/), [BMC 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8364697/)).
- Next-day pain above 5/10: add an extra rest day ([BMC 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8364697/)).
- Next-day pain above the usual baseline: no progression next session **[reasoning]**.

> **Kevin, shoulder: see a doctor if**
> - you feel a sudden pop and then see a **"Popeye" bulge** in the upper arm (possible tendon rupture) ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/biceps-tendinitis/));
> - the pain does not improve with modified training, or improves and then keeps returning ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/biceps-tendinitis/));
> - you get night pain, weakness lifting the arm, or a growing snapping or catching sensation **[reasoning: suggests other shoulder pathology]**.

## 4. Kevin: hip flexor (recurrent, flares with deep squats and running)

**What it might be.** The cause is undiagnosed. Candidates include a recurrent hip flexor strain or iliopsoas tendinopathy, or an intra-articular problem such as femoroacetabular impingement (FAI), which also pinches in deep flexion. The advice below uses AAOS guidance on strains as the primary source and **extrapolates** from FAI guidance for deep-flexion limits. A clinician can tell these apart. JOSPT's 2023 guideline recommends multimodal care built on activity modification plus exercise ([JOSPT hip CPG 2023](https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/jospt.2023.0302.pdf)).

**Mechanism and risk factors.** Strains come from sudden stretch or force or from overuse. Risk factors include **prior injury**, poor warm-up, doing too much too quickly, and fatigue ([AAOS hip strains](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains)). Return gradually: if pain comes back with harder activity, drop back to easier activity ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains)). Talk to a provider before resuming intense activity ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/23978-hip-flexor-strain)).

**Why running and sprinting flare it.** Intramuscular EMG shows iliacus and psoas activity **increasing markedly with running speed** above about 2 m/s ([Andersson, Acta Physiol Scand 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9401589/)). Sprinting is about the most demanding hip-flexor task in a normal training week.

**Why some core work flares it.** The hip flexors are "highly activated only in exercises involving hip flexion": sit-ups that lift the upper body, and leg lifts ([Andersson, Eur J Appl Physiol 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9118976/)). That covers the original program's **hanging leg raises** and the leg-lowering part of a dead bug.

**Deep flexion (extrapolated from FAI guidance).** UK NHS hip physio guidance for FAI advises avoiding or adapting deep hip flexion, such as **deep squatting into full hip flexion or a racing cycling position**. It suggests a **quads-dominant squat** instead, stretching only within a pain-free range, and strengthening the glute max, glute med, deep external rotators and adductors ([British Hip Society / UK Hip Physio Network 2024](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf)).

**Exercise-by-exercise review**

| Exercise | Concern | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Box squat | Depth sets how far the hip flexes | Set the box at or above parallel, at the depth that doesn't pinch, and lower it only once that depth is pain-free. Keep the box at a fixed height (store it in the app). This is a quads-dominant, limited-depth option ([BHS, extrapolated](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf)). |
| Leg press | Deep sled range plus high foot placement means deep hip flexion | Limit depth so the knees don't come toward the chest and the pelvis doesn't tuck. Mid-to-low foot placement. ([BHS, extrapolated](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf)) |
| RDL | A hip-extension exercise that loads the posterior chain; hip flexion is passive | Keep. Hinge to mid-shin or until the hamstrings limit you, with no lumbar rounding **[reasoning]**. |
| Deadlift | Start position is moderately flexed | Keep. Use a conventional stance; raise the bar on blocks if setting up pinches **[reasoning]**. |
| Leg curl, calf raise | Low risk | Keep |
| Dead bug | Leg lowering loads the hip flexors on a long lever ([Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9118976/)) | Use **bent-knee heel taps** (short lever). Progress the lever only while pain-free. |
| Pallof press | Mostly anti-rotation | Keep **[reasoning]** |
| Hanging leg raise, sit-ups (original's ab options) | Heavy hip-flexor recruitment ([Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9118976/)) | **Exclude for Kevin** |
| Cardio | Running and sprinting ([Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9401589/)); a racing bike position is deep flexion ([BHS](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf)) | **No sprints or running intervals.** Use incline treadmill walking, an elliptical, or an upright bike with the seat high. Easy jogging only after weeks of pain-free lifting, and build it gradually ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains)). |

**Warm-up (leg days, about 6 min).** 5 min of easy upright bike or incline walk; then glute bridges 2x10; bodyweight box squats to the working box 1x10; then the original program's ramp sets ([archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/)). Warm-up is an AAOS prevention step ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains)). **No aggressive static hip-flexor stretching before lifting.** The original program advises against static stretching before lifting, and BHS limits stretching to the pain-free range ([archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/), [BHS](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf)).

**Stop rules.** Groin or front-of-hip pinch at the bottom of a squat or leg press: raise the box, or cut the range, by one step. Pain that returns with harder activity: go back to the previous level ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains)).

> **Kevin, hip: see a doctor or physio if**
> - pain persists after a few weeks of home care, or the hip and leg get harder to move ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains)). A doctor may X-ray to rule out a **stress fracture**, which can look like a strain ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains));
> - you get a limp, pain at night or at rest, or catching, locking or clicking deep in the groin **[reasoning: points toward a joint problem rather than a muscle]**;
> - it keeps recurring despite all this. Recurrence is the argument for a formal diagnosis, because FAI and tendinopathy are managed differently ([JOSPT 2023](https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/jospt.2023.0302.pdf)).

## 5. Emily: suspected abdominal strain

**Mechanism.** Abdominal wall strains (rectus abdominis, obliques, transversus) come from direct blows or from **sudden or repetitive trunk flexion and extension or rotation**. Treatment is non-operative, and **symptoms guide** return to activity ([Johnson, Curr Sports Med Rep 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/)). Heavy crunches from a hyperextended position and sitting up fast are both forceful flexion from a stretched position, which fits this mechanism.

**Rectus vs oblique matters for exercise choice.** The app should ask **where** it hurts: a central strip on either side of the navel (rectus) or the side of the waist (oblique).

**Bracing and Valsalva**
- A brief Valsalva manoeuvre is "unavoidable" with heavy loads (above about 80%) or when sets go to failure. Intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) rises with **lifting intensity and effort**. The health risks of the manoeuvre are **unconfirmed** ([Hackett & Chow, JSCR 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23222073/)).
- Takeaway: there's no evidence that bracing causes hernias, so don't make that claim. But **AMRAP sets are the highest-effort, highest-IAP sets in the program**, so they are the right thing to remove while the abdominal wall is sore.
- Cue: take a moderate breath and brace, use a short breath-hold through the hard part of the rep, then exhale. **Sharp abdominal pain while bracing is a stop signal** **[reasoning]**.

**Core exercise review**

| Exercise | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Bird dog | In the quadruped arm and leg lift, only glute max reached the strengthening threshold; unlike the side bridge, it was not flagged as an oblique-strengthening load ([Ekstrom, JOSPT 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18560185/)). The general principle of choosing exercises that challenge muscle while keeping spinal load low comes from McGill ([McGill 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9672547/)), who does not name the bird dog in that abstract. | **Keep.** It's the lowest-load option. |
| Side plank | Strong enough to *strengthen* the external oblique ([Ekstrom 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18560185/)). Rotational side-bridge variants reach about 63% MVIC external oblique and about 44% rectus ([Youdas 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25177418/)). Quadratus lumborum is about 54% active ([McGill 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11415616/)). | **Regress** to a knee (short-lever) side plank of 10-20 s. **Hold it entirely if the pain is on the side of the waist.** No rotational variants. |
| Dead bug | Unilateral leg movement needs little abdominal activation; bilateral leg lifts need much more ([Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9118976/)) | Keep one limb at a time with bent-knee heel taps. **No double-leg lowering.** |
| Pallof press | Anti-rotation, loads the obliques **[reasoning]** | Keep it light. Stop if it is painful on the side of the waist. |
| Crunches, sit-ups, ab wheel, hanging leg raise | Same flexion pattern as the injury ([Johnson 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/)); heavy abdominal recruitment ([Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9118976/)) | **Exclude** for the conservative period |
| Daily life | Sitting up fast was a trigger | Roll onto your side and push up with your arms to get out of bed or off a bench **[reasoning]** |

**Main lifts and cardio**

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Deadlift (115 lb 1x5+) | Keep the weight, **cap at 5 reps (no AMRAP)**, and progress only after a pain-free session and a pain-free next day ([Hackett & Chow](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23222073/); [Johnson](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/)). |
| Barbell row (55 lb 4x5, 1x5+) | Long isometric trunk hold under load. **No AMRAP.** If anything twinges, use a **chest-supported row** for 2-4 weeks. The original allows chest-supported rows as an accessory ([archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/)). |
| Goblet or box squat | Fine. No AMRAP during the conservative period. |
| Bench, OHP | No AMRAP during the conservative period. Use 2.5 lb jumps ([StrongLifts](https://stronglifts.com/stronglifts-5x5/progress/); [ACSM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)). |
| Sprint intervals (10x30 s sprint) | **Hold** for the conservative period. Maximal sprinting drives forceful trunk bracing and rotation **[reasoning; symptom-guided return per [Johnson](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/)]**. |
| 8x(1/2), 4x(4/3) intervals | After 2 pain-free weeks, start with 4x4 at "hard but conversational-ish" pace. Make sprints the last thing to come back. |
| Easy runs | Fine if pain-free during and after |

> **Emily, abdomen: see a doctor if**
> - **non-urgent GP visit:** a **bulge or lump** in the groin, upper thigh or abdomen, especially one that gets bigger when you cough, sneeze or strain and smaller when you lie down; skin over a lump that looks tight; a heavy, dragging feeling in the groin or leg. These suggest a **hernia, not a strain** ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hernia/)). A hernia won't heal on its own the way a strain does.
> - **urgent (NHS 111 or equivalent, same day):** pain in or around a lump, bloating, nausea or vomiting, constipation, fever or shivering, or sudden confusion ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hernia/)).
> - **also:** pain that is not improving or is getting worse after 2-3 weeks of rest from aggravating movements; any pain with fever, vomiting, or blood in stool or vomit. These point to something other than a muscle strain **[reasoning; symptom-guided per [Johnson](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/)]**.
>
> A muscle strain usually feels tender over the muscle and hurts when that muscle contracts. A lump or bulge is the key thing that separates a hernia from a strain ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hernia/)).

**Conservative period:** 2-4 weeks, ending when daily life (getting out of bed, coughing, sneezing) is pain-free. After that, bring back AMRAP first, then the full side plank, then sprints, one at a time.

## 6. Proposed program changes

**Legend:** **Must** = safety item. **Should** = clear benefit, low cost. **Optional** = fidelity or nice-to-have. Items marked **[reasoning]** have no direct source.

### Must

- [ ] **M1. Kevin's deadlift grip is double overhand, then hook grip or straps. Never mixed. Add an in-app warning on the lift.** All the deadlift ruptures in one video study, and 56 of 57 ruptures in another, occurred with the forearm supinated; none occurred with it pronated. ([Kapicioglu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8237209/), [Jukes](https://online.boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/2633-1462.310.BJO-2022-0123.R1))
- [ ] **M2. Kevin: no sprinting or running intervals. His cardio is incline walking, elliptical, or upright bike with the seat high.** Hip-flexor EMG rises sharply with running speed, and running is a known trigger for him. ([Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9401589/), [AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains))
- [ ] **M3. Pain log that overrides progression.** Log pain 0-10 per injured area per exercise.
  - Pain above 3 during a set: stop that exercise and use its regression.
  - Any logged pain, or next-day pain above baseline: **no weight increase** next session.
  - Next-day pain above 5: also add a rest day.
  - Pain-flagged misses don't count toward the 3-fail deload.
  - Rationale: pain-monitored loading is safe and is the evidence-based alternative to rest. ([Silbernagel 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17307888/), [BMC 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8364697/), [Delphi 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159730/))
- [ ] **M4. Emily: AMRAP disabled on all main lifts during the conservative period. Stop at the prescribed 5 reps, leaving about 2-3 in reserve.** IAP rises with effort, and AMRAP sets are the highest-effort sets in the program. ([Hackett & Chow 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23222073/))
- [ ] **M5. Emily: sprint intervals (10x30 s) are held for the conservative period. Bring back 4x4 before 8x1/2, and sprints last.** Return should be symptom-guided. ([Johnson 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/)) **[reasoning on the order]**
- [ ] **M6. Red-flag "see a doctor" boxes for all three injuries, shown in-app (from sections 3, 4 and 5).** Hernia signs need a GP, and the urgent signs need same-day care. Biceps Popeye sign or pop; hip pain persisting past a few weeks. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hernia/), [AAOS biceps](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/biceps-tendinitis/), [AAOS hip](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains))
- [ ] **M7. Emily: exclude crunches, sit-ups, ab wheel and hanging leg raises. Kevin: exclude hanging leg raises and sit-ups.** These are the original program's ab options. They repeat Emily's injury pattern and load Kevin's hip flexors heavily. ([Johnson](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/), [Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9118976/))

### Should

- [ ] **S1. Emily's side plank becomes a knee (short-lever) side plank of 2-3 x 10-20 s per side. Hold it if the pain is on the side of the waist.** The side bridge is a strengthening-level load for the obliques. ([Ekstrom](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18560185/), [Youdas](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25177418/))
- [ ] **S2. Emily's Pull B row: keep 55 lb with no AMRAP, or swap to a chest-supported row for 2-4 weeks if it twinges.** A bent-over row needs a long trunk isometric. The original allows chest-supported rows. ([archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/), [Hackett](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23222073/))
- [ ] **S3. Dead bug for both lifters becomes single-limb bent-knee heel taps. No double-leg lowering.** Long-lever and bilateral leg lifts drive hip-flexor and abdominal load. ([Andersson 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9118976/))
- [ ] **S4. Kevin's box height and leg-press depth are stored settings, starting at or above parallel and changed only when pain-free.** Limiting deep flexion with a quads-dominant squat is the FAI guidance (extrapolated). ([BHS](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf))
- [ ] **S5. Kevin: bench regression to a floor or board press if the bottom position pinches. Incline at 15-30°, stopping with the elbows level with the torso.** Range of motion and start/finish positions are standard shoulder modifications. ([Fees 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9784824/)) **[reasoning on the specific angles]**
- [ ] **S6. Kevin: pronated or neutral lat pulldown grip; overhand rows; no overhead triceps extension or incline curls; hammer curl as the fallback, but keep curls in the program.** These avoid long-head stretch and overhead positions while keeping the biceps progressively loaded. ([Delphi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159730/), [AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/biceps-tendinitis/)) **[reasoning on grips]**
- [ ] **S7. Kevin's shoulder warm-up is specific: band external rotation 2x15, pull-aparts 2x15, curl isometric at 90° 2x30 s.** This matches Delphi consensus on cuff and scapular work and isometric biceps loading. ([Delphi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159730/))
- [ ] **S8. Kevin's hip warm-up: 5 min upright bike or incline walk, glute bridges 2x10, bodyweight box squats 1x10, then ramp sets. No pre-lift static hip-flexor stretching.** Warm-up is an AAOS prevention measure; the original program advises against pre-lift static stretching. ([AAOS](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hip-strains), [archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/))
- [ ] **S9. Emily's bench and OHP use +2.5 lb increments from day 1, not only after stalling.** StrongLifts recommends 2.5 lb for women. +5 lb on 35 lb is about 14%, above ACSM's 2-10%. ([StrongLifts](https://stronglifts.com/stronglifts-5x5/progress/), [ACSM 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/))
- [ ] **S10. Kevin's landmine press MAIN uses +2.5 lb increments, with the AMRAP stopped 2 reps shy.** Plate jumps on a lever arm are coarse, and the shoulder is irritable. **[reasoning]**
- [ ] **S11. Bracing cue in-app for both: moderate brace, short hold through the sticking point, exhale. Sharp abdominal pain while bracing means stop.** Brief Valsalva is normal under heavy load, and its risks are unconfirmed, so cue moderation rather than prohibiting it. ([Hackett & Chow](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23222073/))
- [ ] **S12. Store bar weight per lifter and per lift.** Emily's 35 lb OHP is impossible on a 45 lb bar, so she must be using a lighter or women's bar. The plate math depends on it. **[reasoning]**
- [ ] **S13. Correct the documentation: the original was posted in 2015, and its deadlift progression is +10 lb.** ([Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/metallicadpappl), [archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/))

### Optional

- [ ] **O1. Use 2 sets per accessory for the first 2 weeks, then 3.** Six days a week is above ACSM's 2-3 days for novices, and the original itself suggests cutting to 2 sets when recovery suffers. ([ACSM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/), [archive](https://thefitness.wiki/reddit-archive/a-linear-progression-based-ppl-program-for-beginners/))

## 7. Source quality notes

- **High trust:** AAOS OrthoInfo, NHS, ACSM position stand, JOSPT 2023 hip CPG, peer-reviewed EMG and biomechanics studies (JOSPT, Eur J Appl Physiol, Acta Physiol Scand, Clin Biomech, Sports Health), Silbernagel RCT (AJSM), the British Hip Society / UK Hip Physio Network guidance.
- **Moderate:** the Delphi consensus (expert opinion), the biceps scoping review (it concludes the evidence is thin), and the video-analysis rupture studies (association, not causation; distal not proximal biceps).
- **Program sources:** the Reddit original couldn't be fetched directly. The Fitness Wiki mirror is a near-verbatim archive; Liftosaur is a secondary implementation. StrongLifts details come from its own site.
- **Not retrievable:** PMC11546833 (iliopsoas review) returned a captcha and is not cited. No high-trust consumer page on abdominal muscle strain was found; Johnson 2006 is the only peer-reviewed source here.
- **Checked via search summary only:** the JOSPT 2023 hip CPG ("multimodal care: activity modification plus exercise") and the Cleveland Clinic hip flexor page. Neither page was opened in full.
- **Gaps:** no trial evidence for specific exercise doses in long-head biceps tendinopathy; no direct evidence on landmine press or face-pull tolerance; the hip diagnosis is unknown, so the hip advice is deliberately generic.
