# Emily: running plan (healing → 5K → base → half marathon)

_Written 2026-11-07. Builds on [research.md](./research.md) §5 (abdominal strain) and §8 (decisions). Tags: **[reasoning]** marks the author's own reasoning rather than something a source states._

> **Not medical advice.** This is a training plan drawn from public guidance and the research literature, written to drive an app. It is not a diagnosis or a treatment plan. Emily's abdomen hasn't been formally assessed. The red flags in research.md §5 (a lump or bulge especially) mean stop and see a GP. Get checked before starting if you have any heart, lung or other health concern ([NHS Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/)).

## 1. Summary and principles

### At a glance

| Phase | Weeks | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| Healing | open-ended | Healing mode switched off (daily life pain-free ~2 weeks) |
| 1. Run/walk → 5K | **10** (agreed ~8; see below) | 5K continuous, or 50 min continuous |
| 2. Base | 6 | 75 min easy long run (~5 mi), comfortable |
| 3. Half build | 12 (incl. 2-week taper) | Half marathon (13.1 mi) |

Total: **28 weeks** after healing mode is turned off. Week numbers restart at 1 in each phase. A global week number is the running sum (5K wk 1–10 = global 1–10, base 1–6 = 11–16, half 1–12 = 17–28).

**Default week** (rules in §1.6 decide what happens when lifting days shift):

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Push A + **quality run** | Pull A + easy cardio | Legs + easy cardio | Push B + **easy run** | Pull B + easy cardio | **Long run**, then light legs | Full rest |

Each run is 3 per week with a day between, as in Couch to 5K ([NHS plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/)). Every session adds a **5 min brisk-walk warm-up and a 5 min walk cool-down** (NHS), not counted in the times below.

### Why Phase 1 is 10 weeks, not 8

- NHS Couch to 5K takes a complete beginner from 1 min run / 1.5 min walk to **30 min continuous** in 9 weeks, and says it can take "9 weeks or longer" ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/)). Emily's 2:1 is about NHS week 3–4 level (NHS week 4 is 16 min of running in 21.5 min, about 3:1.3), so she'd reach 30 min continuous around week 6 of an NHS-paced plan.
- But her 5K is **45–55 min**, not 30. Getting from 30 to ~50 min at the long-run cap below (≈10% per step) takes several more weeks.
- She also restarts after a layoff, so week 1 sits a step **below** her old 2:1 ([reasoning]).
- The per-session spike rule (below) gives this long-run path: 25 → 27 → 30 → 33 → (28 cutback) → 36 → 40 → 44 → 48 → 50 min. That lands in week 10. Phases 2 and 3 fit their agreed lengths without change (checked the same way).

### 1.1 Effort guide (prescribe by time and effort, never pace)

| Word in the plan | RPE (0–10) | Talk test | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Easy** | 3–4 | Full sentences, comfortably | Almost all running, all walk-break runs, all non-impact cardio |
| **Steady** | 5–6 | Short sentences; speech is just starting to get harder | Quality-run blocks from Phase 2; roughly half-marathon effort |
| **Hard** | 7–8 | A few words at a time | Only the Phase 3 week 7 4 × 4, and only if cleared |
| **Walk** (in run/walk) | 2 | Easy chat | Brisk, purposeful walking, not strolling |

- The talk test tracks ventilatory threshold: where speech first becomes difficult is almost exactly at threshold, and when speech is uncomfortable the effort is above it ([Persinger 2004, MSSE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15354048/); review: [Reed & Pipe 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25010379/)). So "easy = full sentences" keeps her below threshold.
- The RPE numbers mapped to each talk-test band are **[reasoning]**; the talk test is the primary cue.
- Hal Higdon's Novice 1 half plan uses the same rule: "if you can't talk comfortably, you're running too fast", and walk breaks are allowed any time ([Higdon Novice 1](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/half-marathon-training/novice-1-half-marathon/)).
- At her pace, easy running may feel barely faster than walking. That's correct.

### 1.2 Fresh vs after lifting

- **Long run: always fresh.** Saturday: run first, light legs after.
- **Quality run: fresh if at all possible.** Run it before Push A, or as a separate session. Push A is upper body, so running first costs the lifting little ([reasoning]; order effects in meta-analyses are about *lower-body* strength: [Murlasits 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28783467/), [Eddens 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28917030/)).
- **Easy run: after lifting is fine.** Keep the total time and step down one level:
  - run/walk sessions: drop the run segment to the previous week's ratio, or to **1:1** if that's what feels easy (this is her existing after-lifting habit);
  - continuous runs: same minutes, slower, walk breaks OK.
- If a fresh run/walk session feels hard (RPE above 4 by halfway), switch to the after-lifting version for the rest of it ([reasoning]).

### 1.3 Progression rule (what the evidence actually says)

- **Beginners get hurt more.** A meta-analysis found **17.8** running injuries per 1000 h in novices vs **7.7** in recreational runners ([Videbæk 2015, Sports Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25951917/)).
- **The 10% weekly rule is weakly supported.** In an RCT of 532 novices, a graded 13-week programme built on the 10% rule had the same injury rate as a standard 8-week programme (**20.8% vs 20.3%**) ([Buist 2008, AJSM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/)).
- In a 1-year cohort of 874 novices, injury rates did **not** differ overall between <10%, 10–30% and >30% weekly progression. Those over 30% in two weeks had more *distance-related* injuries (patellofemoral pain, ITB), HR 1.59 (95% CI 0.96–2.66, not significant). The authors call it exploratory ([Nielsen 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25155475/)). This study is in **JOSPT**, not BJSM as the brief said.
- **The clearest signal is single-session spikes.** In 5,205 runners and 588,071 sessions, a run more than 10% longer than the **longest run in the previous 30 days** raised the injury rate: >10–30% HRR 1.64; >30–100% HRR 1.52; >100% HRR 2.28. **Week-to-week ratio showed no relationship** ([Frandsen 2025, BJSM 59(17):1203](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-109380); [record](https://researchportal.lih.lu/en/publications/how-much-running-is-too-much-identifying-high-risk-running-sessio/)). It's observational, so this is association, not cause.

**Rules the plan follows:**
1. **No run more than ~10% longer than her longest run in the last 30 days.** The tables follow this (rounding gives a couple of 11% steps).
2. Frandsen measured **distance**. The plan uses **time** as a stand-in, because at a steady easy effort time and distance move together **[reasoning]**.
3. A cutback week does **not** reset the reference. The next long run is measured against the longest run before the cutback.
4. After the healing break her 30-day reference is effectively zero, so the first long runs restart at or below her old 20–30 min.
5. **Deliberate exceptions: the two goal events.** The Phase 1 5K is capped at 50 min, so it stays within the rule. The **half marathon itself breaks it**: about 3 h against a 2:15 peak is a +30–45% spike, Frandsen's "moderate" band. Every novice plan does this; Higdon goes from a 10 mi longest run to 13.1 mi (+31%). The 2-week taper and run/walk offset some of it **[reasoning]**. The app should not flag race day.

### 1.4 When to repeat a week

NHS says repeating any run or week is fine if you don't feel ready ([NHS plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/)). Repeat the current week if any of these happen **[reasoning]**:

- she couldn't finish a run as written, even with the after-lifting step-down;
- easy runs consistently rated above RPE 4, or she failed the talk test;
- any pain that hits a stop rule (§1.5);
- she missed a run, was ill, or slept badly most of the week.

Missed time:
- missed 1 week → repeat the last completed week;
- missed 2–3 weeks → go back 2 weeks;
- missed 4+ weeks → go back to the start of the phase, or Phase 1 week 1 if the break was injury.

Never skip ahead. Never move a missed long run onto Sunday.

### 1.5 Pain and stop rules

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| **Any abdominal pain while running**, or a lump/bulge | Stop, walk home. Turn healing mode back on for at least 1 week. Bulge or lump = GP (research.md §5, [NHS hernia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hernia/)). |
| Sharp pain, pain that changes your stride, or limping | Stop that run. No running until it's gone; non-impact cardio only if pain-free. |
| Ache ≤3/10 that stays the same or fades during the run and is gone by next morning | OK to continue the plan. |
| Pain >3/10, getting worse during the run, or still there next morning | Skip the next run; repeat the week. Twice in a row → see a clinician. |
| Localised bone pain (one spot on the shin or foot), or pain at rest or at night | Stop running and see a clinician (possible bone stress). |
| Chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness | Stop and seek urgent care. |

The 3/10 limit mirrors the app's lifting rule (research.md §3). It's adapted from pain-monitored loading in Achilles tendinopathy ([Silbernagel 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17307888/)), applied here by analogy **[reasoning]**. NHS: stop if something doesn't feel right.

### 1.6 Leg-day rules (rotation-safe)

Lifting rotates, so the app should apply these **relative to the full leg day**, not to weekdays:

1. **No quality run the day before a full leg day.** It would blunt the squat/RDL session **[reasoning]**.
2. **No quality run on, or the day after, a full leg day.** A single resistance session leaves residual fatigue (neural, movement efficiency, soreness, glycogen) that can impair endurance work **for hours to days** ([Doma 2017, Sports Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28702901/)). The effect depends on intensity, mode, order and recovery ([Doma 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30847824/)). **Easy runs** the day after legs are allowed, since low-intensity running is less sensitive **[reasoning, consistent with Doma 2019]**.
3. **Long run before light legs** on the same day, never after. Squat and RDL stay out of Saturday's session (agreed). The cost: meta-analyses find lower-body strength gains are a bit better when lifting comes first ([Murlasits 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28783467/); [Eddens 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28917030/)). That's why Saturday legs are light and the main leg day stands alone.
4. **If the rotation breaks rules 1–2 for the quality run,** keep the run on its day but make it an easy run of the same length. The table below covers each case.
5. **Running interferes with lifting gains more than cycling does.** Concurrent training with **running, but not cycling**, reduced hypertrophy and strength gains, and more endurance frequency and duration meant more interference ([Wilson 2012, JSCR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22002517/)). Hence: three runs only, and the bike first on non-run days **[reasoning from Wilson]**.
**When a missed day shifts full legs onto a run day** (runs stay on Mon/Thu/Sat; the lifting rotation moves) **[reasoning]**, for each case below:

| Full legs lands on | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Mon** (quality day) | Run first, fresh, as an **easy** run of the same length. No quality run this week. |
| **Tue** | Quality run Monday is the day before legs, so make Monday's run easy (rule 1). |
| **Thu** (easy-run day) | Easy run first, legs after, or split the two by several hours. An easy run can share a leg day; a quality run can't. |
| **Fri** (day before long run) | Do the next upper-body day on Friday and move full legs to the next rotation day instead. If that's impossible, cap Saturday's long run at **last week's long-run time** and keep it easy (Doma's carry-over applies to long runs too). |
| **Sat** (long-run day) | Long run first, then **light** legs only (no squat/RDL). Do the full leg session on the next rotation day after Sunday. Never squat or RDL after a long run. |

6. **Phase 3: cut back lifting as running grows.** Higdon says lifters can continue during a half build but should cut back as mileage builds ([Higdon Novice 1](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/half-marathon-training/novice-1-half-marathon/)). From the first long run over 90 min, drop leg accessories to 2 sets and skip Saturday's light legs on peak weeks if her legs feel heavy **[reasoning]**.

### 1.7 Healing → running: the first runs back

- **Start Phase 1 only when healing mode is off**, i.e. getting out of bed, coughing and sneezing have been pain-free for ~2 weeks (research.md §8).
- **First run:** Phase 1 week 1 easy run (1:1 × 10). Do it fresh, on a flat route or treadmill. Stop at any abdominal pain.
- **Next-morning check:** no abdominal pain and no new tenderness → carry on. Any → back to healing mode for a week, then retry.
- **Return order:** keep research.md's order: AMRAP first, then full side plank, then 4 × 4 intervals, then sprints, one at a time. This plan has **no sprints and no strides**. The only hard session (Phase 3 week 7) is gated on 4 × 4 already being cleared.
- Running is repetitive trunk loading and the strain mechanism includes repetitive trunk motion ([Johnson 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529681/)). So the first two weeks are deliberately below her pre-injury level **[reasoning]**.

### 1.8 Run/walk is a real strategy, not a crutch

In an RCT of 42 non-elite marathoners, run/walk finished in a similar time to running throughout (about 4:14 vs 4:08, not significant) **with less muscle pain and fatigue** afterwards ([Hottenrott 2016, J Sci Med Sport](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25467199/)). Higdon allows walk breaks any time. So "walk breaks OK" on Phase 3 long runs and on race day is a legitimate plan, not a failure. Suggested format: walk 1 min every 10–15 min, or at each water stop **[reasoning]**.

### 1.9 Cutbacks and taper

- **Cutback weeks:** Phase 1 week 5, base week 4, half weeks 4 and 8. Long run drops about 15–25%, other runs shorter, no steady blocks. No high-trust source sets cutback frequency, so this is **[reasoning]**. The nearest support is Higdon: repeated weeks, and lighter race weeks at weeks 6 and 9 ([Higdon](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/half-marathon-training/novice-1-half-marathon/)).
- **Taper:** 2 weeks. Cut volume, keep intensity and frequency. A meta-analysis found the best effect from a **~2-week taper**, with volume cut **41–60%** and intensity and frequency kept ([Bosquet 2007, MSSE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/)). Half week 11 is ~65% of peak-week volume (a 35% cut), and week 12 is ~20% before race day (an 80% cut). That averages about a 57% cut over the two weeks, inside Bosquet's range. Steady segments stay. Higdon's own taper is about 1 week.
- **Race day:** no race date is set. Run it on Saturday of half week 12 (organised race or solo 13.1 mi). If a real race lands later, repeat half week 10, then do the taper ([reasoning]). At her pace the half is likely **~2:55–3:15**, so fuel and fluids matter. Practise them on long runs from half week 6 **[reasoning]**.

## 3. Easy non-impact cardio days

These are the non-run days except Sunday: by default Tue (Pull A), Wed (Legs) and Fri (Pull B). Always **easy** (RPE 3–4, full sentences); none of it is a workout. Order of preference: **bike**, then incline walk, then elliptical. The bike is first because cycling didn't show the strength/hypertrophy interference that running did ([Wilson 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22002517/)) **[reasoning]**. Do it after lifting.

| Phase | Tue (after Pull A) | Wed (after full Legs) | Fri (after Pull B, day before long run) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healing | Incline walk 8% @ 3.8 mph, 20–30 min | Same | Same |
| 1. 5K | 20–30 min easy | 15–20 min easy bike | 15–20 min easy |
| 2. Base | 20–30 min easy | 15–20 min easy bike | 15–20 min easy |
| 3. Half, weeks 1–4 | 20–30 min easy | 15–20 min easy bike | 15 min very easy (RPE 2–3) |
| 3. Half, weeks 5–10 | 20 min easy | 15 min easy bike, or skip if legs are heavy | 10–15 min very easy, or skip |
| 3. Half, taper (11–12) | 15–20 min easy | 15 min easy bike | Skip or 10 min walk |

Rules:
- **Never "hard"** on these days. No intervals. That replaces the app's current push-day intervals.
- **The day before a long run (Fri) is the lightest.** As long runs pass 90 min, it shrinks or drops out **[reasoning]**.
- Elliptical involves trunk rotation. If any abdominal symptom returns, use the bike or incline walk instead **[reasoning]**.
- Sunday: nothing.

## 5. Sources and quality

- **High trust, used directly:** NHS Couch to 5K and plan page; peer-reviewed: Buist 2008 RCT (AJSM), Nielsen 2014 cohort (JOSPT), Frandsen 2025 cohort (BJSM), Videbæk 2015 meta-analysis, Hottenrott 2016 RCT, Wilson 2012, Murlasits 2018 and Eddens 2018 meta-analyses, Doma 2017 and 2019 reviews, Bosquet 2007 meta-analysis, Persinger 2004 and Reed & Pipe 2014 on the talk test.
- **Established plan, not research:** Hal Higdon Novice 1. It's widely used and gives the shape (3 runs plus cross-training, long run to 10 mi, ~1-week taper), but it's a coach's plan, not evidence.
- **Read via abstract or summary only:** most papers were read at abstract level through PubMed. Frandsen 2025 was read via the publisher-linked institutional record (full numbers taken from there). The Doma 2013 next-day-running study was seen only in secondary coverage and is **not** cited.
- **Not retrieved:** Runner's World plans (no primary page found), ACSM running guidance (not located), Mayo Clinic half plan (403).
- **No source found for:** cutback-week frequency, the time-for-distance substitution, the RPE-to-talk-test numbers, and the after-lifting step-down. All are tagged **[reasoning]**.
